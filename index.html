THE PIXELCHIX
PROJECT

Pixel Chix is an ongoing world-building project exploring themes of femininity, technology, and the balance between art and craft through the medium of textile animation.

STYLE_DEVELOPMENT.TXT

Style Development

The style development pulls very directly from the Flash games I played myself when I was younger, as well as from its namesake: the Pixel Chix toy by Mattel.

I never owned the little house with the girl inside myself, but I was captivated whenever I got to play with it at a friend’s house. Here I was, a girl in a room, playing with a little box that housed a pretend girl, a kind of Tamagotchi, but with a room, a personality, hopes, and dreams. The toy created this strange little world within a world: a girl playing with a box containing another girl.

The concept art for Pixel Chix was designed by my friend and collaborator Maya Nguyen. We had many conversations about our shared nostalgia for the toy and the online games we played growing up. Maya grew up in Hanoi and I grew up in Amersfoort, yet these strange little digital worlds connected us. When I asked Maya, sixteen years after the original release of Pixel Chix, to design the characters and layouts for the textile games, they knew exactly what to do.

Together, we wanted to capture the boldness of the early 2000s: the exaggerated femininity, bright colours, glitter, fashion, and slightly chaotic visual language that made these games so recognisable. The style development became a way of translating that feeling into the world of Pixel Chix, while allowing us to bring our own memories and interpretations into it.

TECHNIQUES.TXT

Techniques

I had three different techniques in mind for translating the animation into textiles: felting, quilting, and embroidery. Each technique represents a different part of the world of Pixel Chix and has its own visual and conceptual purpose.

Quilting

The Pixel Chix mini-games are made using quilting, using second-hand clothes and scrap fabric. The fabrics are bold and colourful, and the physical process of cutting out the shapes is reminiscent of another classic girlhood toy: paper dolls.

The mini-games essentially become a form of playing dress-up with textiles. This emulates the idea of play not only within the film’s narrative, but also within the process of making it. The act of cutting, arranging, layering, and dressing the characters becomes another form of play.

Most of the animation, assembly, and compositing happens digitally in After Effects, where the textile elements are brought back together and animated.

Felting

Felting is used to represent the real-world sections of my protagonist, Eyeball. Her world is dreamy, wobbly, and mostly black and white. Eyeball doesn’t feel at home in her own bedroom; instead, she lives vicariously through her digital Pixel Chix, entering a more colourful world where she can become the version of herself she wishes she could be.

To contrast the bright and hyper-feminine world of the Pixel Chix games, Eyeball’s world is grey and made from wool. The animation is felted frame by frame, and the translation process is intentionally inconsistent. The imperfections and slight shifts between frames reflect how out of sync she feels in her own body and her unbalanced state of mind.

Embroidery

The third and final technique is embroidery. This is used to show the Pixel Chix character at home, when she is no longer performing femininity through the textile Flash games. Instead, she returns to her own little Pixel Chix house.

These scenes are made using black-and-white machine embroidery and countless cross-stitches on cotton. The technique references the limited, pixelated graphics of early 8-bit and 16-bit video games, as well as the animations displayed on the LCD screen of the original Mattel Pixel Chix toy.

By using three different textile techniques, I can create a distinction between the different worlds within the film. Felting represents Eyeball’s disconnected reality, quilting represents the colourful and playful world of the mini-games, and embroidery represents the Pixel Chix character herself and her digital home.

NARRATIVE.TXT

Narrative

Pixel Chix observes our protagonist, Eyeball, as she plays her favourite games. Through a series of mini-games, Eyeball escapes the slump of her everyday life and enters a more colourful, glittery world of hyper-femininity. Here, Pixel Chix becomes the avatar through which Eyeball can explore a version of girlhood that feels bolder, more exciting and colourful than her reality.

But as Pixel Chix begins to question her own autonomy and what femininity means to her, a conflict develops between the two. Pixel Chix no longer wants to simply perform the version of femininity that Eyeball has created for her.

In response, Eyeball creates a new avatar: New Girl. She embodies a more traditional idea of femininity and becomes a way for Eyeball to punish Pixel Chix for her rebellious attitude. Faced with being replaced, Pixel Chix realises she needs to escape before she is deleted and replaced by New Girl.

She doesn’t get far before Eyeball catches her. As a final act of cruelty, Eyeball shows Pixel Chix her replacement. But instead of fighting New Girl, Pixel Chix comes to a realization: there is no need to cling to one definition of femininity, not hers, not Eyeball’s, and not New Girl’s. To each their own. It is time to let go.

Eyeballs realises this too, when Pixel Chix hands over her suitcase filled with the mini games to New Girl, The games are passed on to the next generation, giving New Girl the freedom to make them her own.

Pixel Chix wakes up in Eyeball’s felted world. But something has changed. For the first time, the world around her is in colour.

Eyeball sees Pixel Chix in the flesh and is overcome with guilt. She now understands what it means to have kept another version of herself trapped inside a box. Pixel Chix is no longer just an avatar for Eyeball to control; she is another version of herself, a girl who has also been trying to figure out who she is.
But Pixel Chix has already forgiven her. Instead of rejecting Eyeball, she embraces her.

Characters

Eyeball

 Eyeball is the player. Her presence looms throughout the gameplay as creator and god. Through playing these games, she experiences a sense of control that she has not found in her own reality. The online world feels more real to her than the felted world around her.

She creates Pixel Chix as an avatar through which she can live out her own ideas of femininity, while remaining safely behind the screen.

Pixel Chix

 Pixel Chix is Eyeball’s avatar. Over the course of the film, she slowly becomes her own person, questioning the role that Eyeball has created for her.

The femininity that Eyeball has imagined for Pixel Chix becomes a kind of mask, one that Pixel Chix eventually takes off when she returns home to her cross-stitched house. What begins as a character controlled by Eyeball gradually becomes a character with her own desires and sense of self.

New Girl

 New Girl is a work in progress. She is created by Eyeball to replace the rebellious Pixel Chix, embodying a more traditional idea of femininity.

But for now, she is still only a mask: a projection of an older version of what a girl is supposed to be.

SCORE_AND_FOLEY.TXT

Score and Foley

For the score, I collaborated with my friend and fellow artist Segadeath. Their work, as their name suggests, focuses on video game synth style music. Their artist name is a reference to the Japanese computer and arcade game company Sega, which made them feel like a perfect collaborator for this project.

We had previously collaborated on my film Textile Translations: A Labour of Love, where we incorporated sounds recorded from the textile lab and knitting machines into the score. Having worked together before, I knew we would be on the same wavelength for Pixel Chix and could build on that shared language.

For this film, we wanted the score to reflect the nostalgic and slightly surreal world of the games, while still feeling connected to the physicality of the textiles. We also recorded all of the Foley together, creating the sounds of the textile games and the physical actions within them.

The sound became another way of connecting the digital and the handmade: video game synths alongside the tactile sounds of fabric.

ABOUT_THE_TEAM.TXT

About the Team

For Pixel Chix, I collaborated with a multidisciplinary team of animators, designers, web developers, photographers, and textile artists. Maya Nguyen supported the project with creative direction, character design, and background design. Corina Bojan and Jip Franken contributed to the digital animation, Tyra Ang worked on the quilt and website elements, Marin de Wilde developed the website, Kaloyan Iordanov designed the publication, and Mo Futures was responsible for the photography.

Working with this team was an important step in my development as a director. For the first time, I worked with multiple makers simultaneously to create a single artistic world. I discovered that directing does not mean making all the creative decisions yourself, but rather giving others the space to contribute to a shared story from the perspective of their own expertise.
